# Aviation Safety Risk Assessment

This project analyzes historical aviation accident records to identify the **safest aircraft makes and models** for corporate fleet acquisition. The study isolates professional builds over a **40-year operational window (1983–2023)**.

## Core Project Recommendations

The table below breaks down the top recommended aircraft types based on their statistical safety profiles, requiring a minimum baseline of **10 historical incidents** for validation.

| Fleet Category | Recommended Make & Model | Mean Severe Injury Rate | Strategic Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Small Aircraft** | BEECH 1900D | **0.0000** | Perfect occupant preservation records across all historical incident benchmarks. |
| **Small Aircraft** | EMBRAER ERJ190 | **0.0000** | Resilient structural survival profile under varying airport configurations. |
| **Large Passenger** | BOEING 737-800 | **0.0125** | High structural crashworthiness and reliable emergency evacuation setups. |
| **Large Passenger** | AIRBUS A320 | **0.0184** | Multi-engine redundancy consistently limits severe human injury rates. |

## Causal Risk Factors Analysis

We evaluated **Number of Engines** to determine their direct impact on aircraft damage and survival outcomes.

| Risk Variable Analyzed | Safety Performance Metric | High-Risk Group Profile | Low-Risk Group Profile | Analytical Takeaway & Causal Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Engines** | Mean Severe Injury Fraction | **1 or 2 Engines** (~0.25) | **3 or 4 Engines** (<0.08) | Multi-engine redundancy minimizes the likelihood of uncontrolled emergency glide landings. |
